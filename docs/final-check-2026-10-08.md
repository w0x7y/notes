## Remaining findings, ordered by severity

1. **P1, existing local filesystem boundary: intermediate-directory symlink substitution.** Native containment validation and later path-based open/create/rename/Trash are separate operations. `O_NOFOLLOW` protects the final component, while a concurrent local process can replace an ancestor after validation. The mutation mutex orders app operations only. This can redirect access outside the registered root. Next action: descriptor-relative root/path traversal and mutation operations (`openat`/`renameat` or a compatible capability filesystem abstraction), with adversarial barrier regressions. Re-canonicalizing alone cannot eliminate the race. No live exploit was attempted.
2. **P2, existing external-writer race:** another program can change a note between revision checking and atomic replacement. Application writes remain ordered and optimistic checks reject observed stale revisions, but cross-process compare-and-replace is not guaranteed; an external change in that interval can be lost. Next action: design a cross-process coordination/recovery policy and test it with temporary files. The Keep mine repair does not claim to close this interval.
3. **P2 privacy: HTTPS images still load automatically.** A note can disclose the user's IP and open time to its image host despite `no-referrer`. HTTP blocking does not resolve this. Next action: a remote-image opt-in preference with a load placeholder, preserving local images. This remains unchecked in TODO.

Local GLib/macro backport upkeep and the unimplemented feature/performance backlog remain in TODO. Current GTK3 maintenance wording was corrected: [RUSTSEC-2024-0415](https://rustsec.org/advisories/RUSTSEC-2024-0415.html) is withdrawn after development resumed, last modified 2026-09-08; [RUSTSEC-2024-0413](https://rustsec.org/advisories/RUSTSEC-2024-0413.html) identifies `atk`. Neither removes the need for the three local patches. Historical dated reports retain their original conclusions.
